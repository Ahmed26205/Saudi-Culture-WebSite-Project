Saudi Traditions

Saudi traditions appear clearly in daily life: in hospitality, greetings, clothing, and celebrations such as Eid and weddings. This page gives a short overview of key social customs in the Kingdom.

Hospitality and Saudi Coffee

Hospitality is a core value in Saudi society. Guests, including unexpected visitors, are usually welcomed warmly and offered coffee and dates before a main meal.

  • Serving Arabic coffee first, often with dates or sweets.
  • Insisting that the guest feels at home and well taken care of.
  • Preparing generous meals on special visits and occasions.

Greetings and Social Etiquette

Greetings may differ from one region to another, but they all reflect respect and strong family and community ties.

  • Common greetings include “Assalamu Alaikum” and asking about family members.
  • Men may shake hands or lightly hug close relatives and friends.
  • Conversations usually start with polite small talk before any business topic.

Traditional Dress and Modesty

Dress in Saudi Arabia is generally modest and influenced by local customs and climate. Traditional clothing is still worn daily and on special occasions.

  • Many men wear a white thobe, and a darker thobe in winter.
  • Women usually wear an abaya in public, with styles that vary by region.
  • Visitors are expected to dress modestly in public places.

Celebrations and Special Occasions

Religious and social occasions highlight Saudi customs, family gatherings, and traditional joy.

  • Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha: praying in congregation, visiting relatives, and exchanging festive meals and sweets.
  • Eidiya: giving money or gifts to children on the morning of Eid.
  • Henna nights before weddings, where women gather to celebrate and decorate the bride’s hands with henna.
